# Responsible Use and Ethics

Automated toxicity classification can reduce moderator workload, but it can also
silence legitimate speech or disproportionately affect dialects and identities.

## Principles

1. Use predictions to prioritize review, not to replace human judgment.
2. Provide affected users with understandable rules and an appeal path.
3. Evaluate false positives and false negatives across relevant subgroups.
4. Minimize collection, display, and retention of raw user comments.
5. Avoid inferring protected traits from text.
6. Document datasets, thresholds, model versions, and known limitations.
7. Revalidate after platform, language, policy, or population changes.

## Content Warning

Real toxicity datasets can include abusive, hateful, threatening, or otherwise
distressing material. Access should be voluntary, role-limited, and supported by
clear handling procedures. Repository tests use a small synthetic fixture only.

## Human Oversight

Moderation policy should define who reviews flags, how context is considered,
when no action is appropriate, and how decisions can be challenged. Model scores
must not be presented as objective truth.
